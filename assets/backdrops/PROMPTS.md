# Backdrop prompts

Ten backdrops, two of each kind. Generate at **2560x1440**, save as jpg or png into
this folder, and they appear in the editor's Look panel automatically. The filename
becomes the label, so `warm-dune.jpg` reads as "Warm Dune".

## The one rule that matters

The recording sits on top of these with an inset and a shadow. **The middle 70% must
stay quiet.** Put interest in the corners and edges. Anything busy, high contrast or
detailed in the centre will fight the video and make text inside the recording
unreadable. Every prompt below ends with a constraint enforcing that, and it is the
part not to edit out.

Shared suffix, append to all ten:

> Landscape 16:9, 2560x1440. No text, no letters, no logos, no watermark, no UI,
> no devices, no people. The central 70 percent of the frame is calm, low contrast
> and nearly uniform, with all visual interest pushed to the outer edges and corners.
> Even, soft lighting. No vignette so dark it reads as a frame.

---

## Gradients (2)

**1. `warm-dune`**
> A smooth diagonal gradient running from warm amber at the top left through burnt
> caramel to deep espresso brown at the bottom right. Colours blend with no visible
> banding, like a large-format studio backdrop. Very slight film grain. Nothing else
> in the frame.

**2. `cold-harbour`**
> A smooth gradient from pale slate blue at the top through steel grey-blue to near
> black at the bottom. Cool, calm and even, with a faint horizontal light bloom near
> the upper third, like light through fog. No banding, no shapes.

## Sketch (2)

**3. `blueprint-grid`**
> A technical blueprint drawing: fine white line work on a deep navy ground. Precise
> thin grid lines, small measurement ticks, a few compass arcs and dimension marks
> gathered along the left and right edges only. Hand-drafted engineering feel, uniform
> line weight, no shading.

**4. `paper-doodle`**
> Loose pencil sketch on warm off-white paper. Light graphite arrows, small circles,
> underlines and margin scribbles drifting in from the outer corners, the way notes
> collect around the edge of a page. Visible paper tooth. Faint, low contrast, as if
> drawn with a 2H pencil.

## Editorial (2)

**5. `press-column`**
> Editorial magazine spread aesthetic: a cream paper ground with a single thin dark
> rule running down the far left and far right margins, generous empty space between
> them. Muted ink black and warm grey. Restrained, Swiss, print-like. No type, only
> the rules and the paper.

**6. `gallery-wall`**
> A photographed gallery wall in soft museum lighting. Plaster in a warm bone white
> with subtle surface texture, a faint shadow gradient falling in from the top corners,
> and a barely visible horizontal picture rail high in the frame. Quiet, expensive,
> architectural.

## Plain, two different kinds (2)

**7. `flat-ink`**
> A single flat solid colour: very dark warm charcoal, almost black with a brown
> undertone. Absolutely uniform, no gradient, no texture, no light source. The look of
> a matte painted studio wall shot straight on.

**8. `linen-bone`**
> A plain woven linen surface in bone white, shot flat from above. Uniform natural
> fibre texture across the whole frame, no pattern, no seams, no folds. Soft diffuse
> light. Plain in the material sense rather than the flat colour sense.

## Depth and material (2)

**9. `soft-forms`**
> Large out-of-focus rounded 3D shapes in matte clay: a sphere and a soft cube resting
> at the bottom left and top right corners, rendered in dusty terracotta and warm sand.
> Shallow depth of field so the shapes read as soft blurred masses. Studio light from
> above.

**10. `deep-space`**
> A dark cosmic field: near-black indigo with a faint dust nebula glowing violet and
> teal along the bottom edge and upper left corner. Sparse, tiny, evenly scattered
> stars. Deep and atmospheric, with the centre almost entirely empty black.

---

## Choosing them

- 1, 7 and 10 flatter dark app recordings
- 5, 6 and 8 suit light UI recordings and read as premium
- 3 and 4 give personality for developer and product demos
- 2 and 9 sit in between and work for most things

Generate all ten, drop them in, then try each against one real recording. The ones that
survive that test are the ones to ship.
